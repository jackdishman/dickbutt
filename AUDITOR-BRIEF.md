## Trust and authority

- A proposer (bot key, or the owner implicitly) can propose arbitrary Merkle roots. The contract enforces proof membership, solvency, a per-round share cap, a proposal rate limit and a guardian pause -- not fair allocation or holder eligibility. The proposer cannot directly call the keeper-only payout method. The keeper independently recalculates historical eligibility and payouts; checking only a copied proposer journal was demonstrated to be insufficient. The owner can still appoint keepers and propose roots. The selected deployment explicitly sets the review delay to zero, although the constructor retains a 24-hour default. Zero permits immediate permissionless activation and removes the guaranteed guardian cancellation window. Existing pending rounds keep their original readyAt. Audit this changed policy and the independent keeper before deployment. Exclusions, weighting and thresholds remain off-chain policy.
- The share cap is also an economics decision: below 100% it permanently defers part of each pot. Review `maxRoundBps` against the intended payout schedule, and review the calculator's cap handling, which bounds new shares and pays proportional capped instalments while retaining unpaid credit. A cap-limited transfer may be smaller than the eligibility payout threshold.
- A floor setter (bot key) refreshes a positive floor expiring within one day, and may not set it below the owner's `floorLowerBound`. It is not an oracle and can be stale even before expiry. A keeper can tighten the floor but cannot weaken it. Keeper and floor setter are mutually exclusive on-chain. A compromised floor setter can set the floor at the bound; a compromised owner can do anything; a missing refresh stops swaps. Review the bound's meaning: the owner's judgement of a price below which swaps should halt rather than execute.
- Harvest destinations can be timelocked/frozen. Freezing them does not remove distributor, token issuer, upstream Safe or swap dependencies.
- The legacy adapter's module, token, destination and Safe list are immutable. Assigning it creator authority is permanent because it has no relay to update the creator. Review migration/recovery policy and future-sink risk **before production assignment**. Rehearsal does not imply approval of this permanent choice.
- Safe owners can disable the legacy module. The creator role does not control the Safe or WETH fees. Locker ownership and legacy creator identity are separate handoffs.
- Native B20 SPCXc may be paused or policy-restricted by its issuer. Test behavior with Base's native implementation. Never replace its bytecode with a mock and report the result as native compatibility.

## Specific review targets

1. Splits factory authenticity, clone bindings, immutable config hash, recipient order/allocations, zero incentive, and Warehouse/direct balances. Constructor introspection cannot authenticate a malicious factory supplied by the deployer. Use the recorded official address and code evidence.
2. Dust retention and atomic fee distributions. The fee Split can revert on one blocked recipient; the rewards distributor instead catches individual recipient transfer failures. Unexpected tokens sent to the narrow router cannot be recovered.
3. Swap path generation, two-hop signed tick spacings, actual output/input deltas, temporary allowances, amount cap, floor rounding, deadlines, keeper authorization, the floor-setter role and its bound, and the keeper/floor-setter exclusion. Fee allocation happens once, in Splits.
4. Pending/active reservations, partial payments, early close/cancel reconciliation, duplicate recipients, forged roots and cross-round replay. Also the proposer/guardian split: share-cap arithmetic against unreserved balance, rate limiting that a cancellation must not refund, pause that never strands committed rounds, the guardian following an ownership transfer unless split, and role authorisation boundaries.
5. Calculator event completeness, finalized-boundary consistency, the bootstrap period and eligibility start time, mint/burn accounting, journal hash/config verification, backup and truncation limits, and explicit weighting. Also the supersede path: a closed foreign round at a planned id recredits the plan exactly once, while a pending or active one stops the run. Linear is rehearsal policy; square-root allocations are Sybilable.
6. Keeper signer locks and the bounded lock wait, unknown transaction handling, proof/config rebuilding, on-chain commitments and snapshot hashes, `foreign-commitment` and `superseded` handling that leaves other rounds moving, timelock checks, retry without double payment, and no automatic early closure. A local lock cannot prevent independent machines from using the same key.
7. Aerodrome basic factory registry, pool/pair identity, volatile flag, effective fee, ERC-20 LP share, prior-holder fee entitlement, unstaked custody, additional LP contributions and lock recovery. Check the real newly created pool before handoff. Historical Slipstream manager/NFT tests do not prove this candidate's production configuration.
8. Production recipients, funding, proposer workflow, source discovery and permanent custody changes. The rehearsal operating CLIs reject mainnet writes; do not remove that restriction without a separate production operating review.
